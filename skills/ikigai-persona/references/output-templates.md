# Output Templates

Read this before writing the final deliverable. The templates are a floor, not a ceiling
— drop sections that would be empty rather than padding them, and add anything the
person's situation demands.

## The standard to hit

Before you send it, run three checks:

**The name test.** Swap in a different person's name. If it still reads as true, you
have written a horoscope. Rewrite with the specifics they gave you.

**The traceability test.** Every claim should be traceable to something they said. Where
you inferred rather than heard, mark it — "I'm reading between the lines here" — so they
can reject it.

**The Monday test.** Could they do something differently tomorrow because of this? If
not, the experiments section is too abstract.

Use their vocabulary throughout. If they said "grunt work", write grunt work, not
"low-value operational tasks." The document should sound like it was written by someone
who was actually listening.

## 1. Persona

```markdown
# Persona: [Name] — [short archetype phrase in their own terms]

**In one line:** [who they are and what they're wrestling with, plainly]

## Where they are now
[Role, situation, what triggered this conversation, what's fixed and what's movable.]

## What gives them energy
- [Specific thing] — evidence: [the episode they described]

## What drains them
- [Specific thing] — evidence: [what they said]

## Signature strengths
- **[Strength]** — evidence: [external proof: who said it, what they were picked for]
- **[Buried strength they discount]** — evidence: [...]

## Who they want to be useful to
[The specific group, in their words, and why this group.]

## Real constraints
[Money floor, dependents, location, visa, health, time. Facts, not fears.]
[Note separately anything they treat as fixed that may not be.]

## Values in tension
[The genuine trade-off they're living. E.g. autonomy vs. security, craft vs. reach.]
[Say which way they leaned when it came up, if they did.]

## In their words
> "[Verbatim quote]"
> "[Verbatim quote]"
> "[Verbatim quote]"
```

The quotes are not decoration. They are what makes a person read the document and think
*that's me* rather than *that's a template*. Pull the lines where their language got
vivid, unguarded, or unusually blunt.

## 2. Ikigai map

```markdown
# Ikigai Map: [Name]

## The four
**Love:** [3–5 items, their words]
**Good at:** [3–5 items, with the evidence]
**World needs:** [the specific people and problem]
**Paid for:** [what's proven, what's plausible, what's unknown]

## The overlaps
**Passion (love + good at):** [...]
**Mission (love + world needs):** [...]
**Profession (good at + paid for):** [...]
**Vocation (world needs + paid for):** [...]

## Where you're standing now
[Name the overlap they currently occupy and what that explains about how they feel.]

## The centre
[The combination where all four hold — skill + who + arrangement. Not a job title.]

## The thin quadrant
[Which is weakest and what would strengthen it. Be direct.]

## Open questions
[Anything you'd need to know or they'd need to research. Don't guess salaries.]
```

Naming which overlap they currently occupy is frequently the highest-value line in the
whole document. It explains a feeling they have carried for years.

## 3. Purpose statement

Give both versions and let them keep whichever fits.

**Structured:**
> I use **[strengths]** to help **[specific who]** **[specific change]**, because
> **[why it matters to them personally]**.

**Raw:** one sentence in their own voice — what they would say to a friend in a pub, not
a mission statement. Build it from their actual phrasing.

Two rules. The "who" must be specific enough to picture: "junior engineers at their
first startup", not "people". And the "because" must come from something they told you
about their own life, not from generic virtue — that clause is what makes it survive a
bad week.

## 4. Experiments

Two or three. Each needs a time-box, a first action small enough to start this week, and
a signal to watch for. The signal is the part people skip and the part that makes an
experiment an experiment.

```markdown
### Experiment 1: [name it]
**Question it answers:** [the specific uncertainty]
**Do this:** [concrete first action, doable within a week]
**Time-box:** [e.g. three weekends, one month]
**Watch for:** [what tells you yes; what tells you no]
**Cost if it fails:** [should be small — that's the point]
```

Good experiments are reversible, cheap, and produce information regardless of outcome.
"Apply for jobs in X" is not an experiment; "have coffee with three people doing X and
ask what their worst week looks like" is. Bias toward tests that expose someone to the
*daily texture* of a path rather than its highlight reel — most career regret comes from
falling in love with the outcome and discovering you hate the Tuesday.

## Worked example

The difference between output that helps and output that doesn't.

**Generic (do not do this):**

> **Persona:** Priya is a creative problem-solver who values authenticity and impact.
> She's passionate about technology and helping others, and seeks meaningful work that
> aligns with her values.
>
> **Purpose:** To leverage my unique talents to make a positive impact on the world.
>
> **Next step:** Explore careers that align with your passions.

Everything there is true of roughly four billion people. It cost her an hour and gave
her nothing.

**Evidence-backed (do this):**

> **Persona: Priya — "the one who translates"**
>
> **In one line:** A backend engineer of nine years who is bored by the code and comes
> alive when she's the only person in the room who can explain it, currently deciding
> whether that means management or something else entirely.
>
> **Energy:** Onboarding new engineers — described the two weeks pairing with a new grad
> as "the only time last year I didn't check the clock." Writing the internal docs
> nobody asked her to write. Explaining the migration to the finance team.
>
> **Drains:** On-call. Estimation meetings. "Being the person who knows where the bodies
> are buried in a codebase I didn't choose."
>
> **Strengths:** Translation between technical and non-technical — evidence: finance and
> legal both route questions to her rather than to her manager, and she's been pulled
> into three customer calls this year despite having no customer-facing role. She
> dismissed this as "just explaining things", which is the tell.
>
> **Who she wants to serve:** Career-changers entering engineering — she switched from
> biology at 27 and said "nobody told me the first six months feel like fraud."
>
> **Constraints:** Needs £58k floor (mortgage, sole income). Six months of runway. Not
> movable this year.
>
> **Standing in:** Profession — good at it, paid well, and the love and world-needs
> quadrants are both empty. That's the whole feeling.
>
> **Purpose (structured):** I use my ability to translate technical complexity into
> plain language to help career-changers survive their first year in engineering,
> because I remember exactly how alone that felt at 27.
>
> **Purpose (raw):** "I want to be the person nobody had when I switched."
>
> **Experiment 1: Is teaching the thing, or is being needed the thing?**
> Run four sessions of an internal "first 90 days" clinic for new joiners. Time-box: six
> weeks. Watch for: whether you're still preparing carefully by session four, or whether
> you're bored once the novelty of being asked wears off. Cost if it fails: four hours
> and a Slack message.
>
> **Experiment 2: Does this pay?**
> Talk to three developer-relations and three bootcamp-instructor people about money and
> their worst week. Time-box: one month. Watch for: whether any path clears £58k without
> requiring you to become a salesperson. Cost if it fails: six coffees.
>
> **Open question:** DevRel salary ranges in the UK at your seniority — I don't have
> reliable figures and guessing here could cost you a year.

Note what the second version does: every claim carries its evidence, the buried strength
is flagged as buried, the constraint is a number, the experiments have failure signals,
and the thing nobody knows is labeled as unknown rather than filled in with a confident
guess.
